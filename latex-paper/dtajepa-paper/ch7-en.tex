\section{STLS-JEPA: Thresholded Allocation, Straightened Latents}\label{sec:stls}

The diagnostics of \S\ref{sec:results} are actionable, and acting on them is a better use of the
remaining budget than tuning the existing knobs. We therefore derive \textbf{STLS-JEPA}
(Table~\ref{tab:stls}) from four measured failures of DTA-JEPA. Nothing else changes: same
encoder, same dual-timescale predictor, same planner, same episode sets.

\subsection{Latent straightening}

Pretraining adds a trajectory-shape term to the two-term objective,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{str}}=\tfrac1d\Big[\big\|z_{t+1}-2z_t+z_{t-1}\big\|_2^2
+\big\|\hat y_{t+1}-2z_t+z_{t-1}\big\|_2^2\Big],
\end{equation}
the first bracket through the encoder (the encoded window should itself be straight) and the
second through the predictor (the next predicted latent should continue that line). This is the
temporal-straightening criterion of Wang et al.\ \cite{wang-2026-temporal}, which AdaJEPA's base
model uses and which our version omitted: we regularised the latent \emph{distribution} (SIGReg)
but never the latent \emph{geometry}. A straight latent trajectory makes the quadratic goal cost
a faithful proxy for the true objective, which is what a ten-step Adam planner needs.

\subsection{Thresholded, calibrated allocation}

Let $e^\top\Sigma^{-1}e$ be the diagonal Mahalanobis residual of an observed transition. We fit a
scalar temperature on held-out in-distribution transitions,
$\tau=\mathbb E[e^\top\Sigma^{-1}e]$, so that
$s_{\mathrm{cal}}=(e^\top\Sigma^{-1}e)/\tau$ has mean one on data the model was trained for, then
allocate against quantiles of that calibrated distribution:
\begin{equation}
U_t=1+\mathbb 1[s_{\mathrm{cal}}>q_{75}]+\mathbb 1[s_{\mathrm{cal}}>q_{95}],
\end{equation}
with $q_{75},q_{95}$ the corresponding quantiles of $s_{\mathrm{cal}}$ on the reference stream, and where the reference stream is the \emph{closed-loop} surprise of a no-update probe of the same model rather than an open-loop teacher-forced stream. Two implementation details mattered more than the choice of quantiles, and we record them because they are the kind of defect that silently invalidates an allocation policy: the surprise handed to the controller must be normalised by the \emph{predictive} variance (an early version passed the raw squared error, and, separately, the Monte-Carlo \emph{epistemic} spread, neither of which is the quantity the temperature was fitted on), and that variance must actually reach the controller (the call site hard-coded it to zero). With both fixed, the closed-loop reference gives $\tau=1.54$ against $1.09$ open-loop --- a modest difference, so the decisive defect was the quantity, not the stream.
Unlike \S\ref{sec:ctrl}, the reference is external (a fixed quantile) rather than the
controller's own lagging moving average, so the allocation cannot drift to its ceiling merely
because surprise rises within an episode. The fitted temperature is informative about \emph{which} half of the fix carries the weight:
$\tau=1.09$ (push family) and $1.37$ (maze family), i.e.\ rescaling is nearly a no-op because the
global variance scale was already right ($1.13$--$1.19$ raw). What was wrong is the
\emph{reference}, not the scale: a threshold on a fixed external quantile replaces a ratio
against the controller's own lagging moving average. The temperature is therefore kept as a
diagnostic --- $\tau\gg1$ would indicate a genuinely mis-scaled head --- while the quantile
thresholds do the work.

\subsection{Curvature-gated refinement}

Depth is spent only where the trajectory is not already straight:
\begin{equation}
K_t=1 \quad\text{if}\quad \big\|z_t-2z_{t-1}+z_{t-2}\big\|^2\le \mathrm{median}_{\mathcal D},
\qquad\text{otherwise the quantile rule above, capped at three.}
\end{equation}
This is the direct response to the dynamics-shift counterexample: when the operator itself is
wrong, iterating it amplifies error, and the right currency is adaptation (larger $U_t$), not
recursion.

\subsection{Stability with a calibrated trust region}

The anchor radius becomes $\epsilon_f=c\,\eta_f\sqrt{n_f}\,\bar U\bar R$, with $c=0.5$ and
$\bar U,\bar R$ the expected per-episode update count and replanning depth, so that it bounds the
\emph{cumulative} drift of an episode rather than the displacement of one step; with
$n_f=594.7$k and $\eta_f=5\times10^{-4}$ this gives $\epsilon_f\approx8$ in place of $0.8$.

\subsection{Falsifiable expectations}\label{sec:stlsexp}

We pre-register the four consequences in Table~\ref{tab:stls}: if STLS does not move the step
count, the projection count or the success curves, then the diagnoses were right and the repairs
were not --- which is itself the reportable outcome. Results follow in \S\ref{sec:stlsres}.
